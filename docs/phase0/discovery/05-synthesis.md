# Desk-Research Discovery: Gate A Synthesis (Phase 0, 2026-10-05)

**Inputs:** 50 public-evidence cases in `cases/` (20 builder, 20 fleet, 10 vendor; two research passes), the GitHub issue corpus (`07-github-issue-corpus.md`, 113 issues across 14 trackers), the survey and paper digest (`06-quantitative-evidence.md`), and the method and limits note (`04-desk-research-method.md`). Counters are in `00-counters.md` and are regenerated by `scripts/tally_discovery.py`.

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The public record confirms the *mechanism* we are betting on: harness components, not the model, are the attributed cause in almost every failure anyone has written up, harness changes regress behaviour silently, and detection is a human watching a terminal. It confirms it most strongly from the vendors' own postmortems, which also show that well-funded vendors answer by building evaluation gates for *their* harness in-house. The customer-side gap is real and unoccupied: nobody gates a customer's own rules files, hooks, permission policies, MCP configuration or model defaults, nobody can reproduce a failed run, and coding-agent traces reach a third-party stack only through Datadog, which launched a fleet-monitoring product in June 2026. What the public record cannot establish is willingness to pay, willingness to host replay, and design-partner intent. Those three counters are reported as not evaluable and move to the first weeks of Phase 1 as live conversations; no PLAN-09 kill trigger fires on the evidence that is observable.

## 1. Findings by interview question

### Q1–Q5 Last failure, detection, time to why, attribution, recurrence
- **Attribution is non-model almost everywhere it is stated.** In 26 of the 28 cases with any attribution the named component is context, verification, environment, permission, prompt, budget or compaction; the two model attributions are Microsoft .NET, where the cause was model *routing* fixed by editing the rules file, and Amp, a vendor model update caught in use. In the GitHub corpus 5 of 113 issues are classed model, and those are disputed by maintainers.
- **Vendors' own root causes are harness components.** Anthropic's April 2026 postmortem traces a quality regression to a default effort change, a context-pruning bug and one system-prompt line, all of which passed internal evals and were reported by users first. Codex's degradation traces to compaction. Both are grade A or B primary evidence (`vendor-01`, `vendor-02`).
- **Detection is a human watching.** In the corpus 62% of failures were seen live by the user, 14% found later by `git diff` or missing data, 10% by cost or quota exhaustion, and none by CI or a regression gate. Uber found its cost incident by budget exhaustion; Elastic found a hook that never ran only after weeks of CI timeouts; the Railway database wipe (`fleet-19`) recurred eleven days after the first incident because nothing turned the first into a guard.
- **Time to why is long and manual where it is stated.** Codex issue 18720 describes two weeks of blaming the model before finding compaction; Anthropic's postmortem describes weeks between user reports and a named cause; the papers put automated step-level localization at 11–24% accuracy.

### Q6–Q8 Volumes, failure rates, invisible failures
- Volumes are public and large: Uber about 60,000 agent tasks a week, Stripe over 1,300 unattended PRs a week, Spotify over 650 PRs a month, Monzo about 1,800 tasks a day, Lyft's support agent in the hundreds of thousands of conversations, Clay hundreds of millions of runs a month. Failure *rates* per production run are not published by anyone; surveys use five incompatible definitions.
- **Invisible failures are a documented class**, not a hypothesis: instructions truncated without warning, permission rules dropped with no log line, hooks that stop firing, subagents bypassing deny rules, fabricated edit successes, cost under-reported, checkpoints never flushed (corpus §"Failures nobody sees"). These map directly onto SPEC-03 detectors and are the seed list for Phase 1.

### Q9–Q12 Harness composition, changes, silent regressions, controls owned
- **Silent regressions after harness changes are stated in nine cases** (Anthropic, Codex, Amp, Amazon, Microsoft .NET, Elastic, Lyft, Shopify, AutonomyAI) and in 30 of 113 corpus issues, 18 of them tied to a harness release. The corpus cannot see regressions users cause with their own rules-file or hook edits, because nobody files a vendor issue for those; that blind spot is exactly the customer-side gap.
- **Eval-gated change exists in a minority of builders** (monday.com CI-gated offline evals, Lyft CI prompt linting, BlackRock daily CI evals, Nubank CI-gated skill vetting), always on synthetic or golden sets, never on production failures, and never covering the closed-harness surface.
- **Harness change is frequent and ungated on the customer side.** Microsoft's rules file for the Copilot agent changed 27 times in 17 months and each fix was validated by hand across three models; Spotify says it has no structured way to tell which prompt or model performs better; Salesforce ships one SDK over two swappable harnesses that expose different telemetry and can silently drop stream content after an upgrade; Sentry's permission allowlist has only ever widened; Brex's only check is for stale documentation.
- **Every A- or B-grade fleet has a central owner of the harness surface** (platform or developer-experience team owning rules files, hooks, MCP gateway, allowlists, model default, budgets): Uber, Stripe, Spotify, Datadog, Elastic, Coinbase, NVIDIA, Brex, Doctolib, Delivery Hero. The buyer we described in PLAN-07 exists.
- **Eval-gated harness change exists where the operator is also an evaluation vendor or a harness vendor.** Datadog runs 140-plus nightly evals and A/B cohorts before changing Claude Code defaults and accepted a measured 8% quality loss for 36.7% lower cost; Gemini CLI publishes a CI workflow that runs behavioural evals when prompts, tools or agent code change; Cursor and GitHub run model-change boards. Nubank built a held-out benchmark by hand before scaling Devin on one migration. No ordinary fleet operator describes a gate on its own harness changes.

### Q13–Q17 Tooling, trace routing, reproduction, cohorts, most-wanted question
- Coding-agent traces reach some trace store in 21 of 50 cases, but almost always an in-house gateway (Uber, Cloudflare, Delivery Hero, Box, LinkedIn, Brex), MLflow (Spotify), LangSmith (Coinbase self-hosted, monday.com, Lyft), Logfire (AutonomyAI), Braintrust (Ramp) or Temporal (Duolingo). The only stated third-party path is Datadog, and Datadog is the vendor. Capability is universal (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Copilot and Factory export OTLP); routing is rare.
- **Nobody states they can reproduce a failed run.** goose issue 12043: "There is no documented way to reproduce a goose session after the fact … Re-running the same prompt produces a different session." Cohort comparison exists only as model A/B or grounded-versus-ungrounded studies, never failed-versus-succeeded runs.
- The most-wanted question, where one is voiced, is attribution: "was it the model or something we changed" (Anthropic's customer reports, Codex 18720, Microsoft .NET).

### Q18–Q21 Data constraints, replay hosting, budget owner, automation limits
- **Content must stay with the customer** is stated by vendors who sell to enterprises: Factory sends content spans only to the customer's collector and offers an airgapped mode; GitHub and Anthropic keep content off by default; Cisco CX moved agent workloads on-prem on data-sovereignty grounds; League keeps patient data where the model has no access. Anthropic's postmortem says its own privacy controls stopped engineers from seeing the problematic interactions. This is the design constraint behind customer-side replay (PLAN-11 D-033) and it is confirmed, but no source answers whether a third party may run replay inside the environment.
- Budget owner, where inferable, is developer productivity or AI platform; surveys say AI spend has moved from innovation budgets to central IT and business units. Contract sizes are not public anywhere.
- Automation limits: Uber caps spend per employee per tool; Stripe caps agents at two CI rounds; NVIDIA logs every allow and deny in a policy sandbox. Pause or stop on evidence is stated nowhere; three fleets have structures that imply it is acceptable.

### Q22–Q24 Reaction, design partnership, referrals
Not observable. The case files name public owners (talks, blog authors, PR authors) as the first outreach list for Phase 1.

## 2. Gate A counters
See `00-counters.md` for the generated table. Reading it against PLAN-04 and PLAN-09 §6:
- **Pain counter.** Stated silent regressions: nine cases, all A or B grade, plus the corpus; one stated willingness-to-pay signal (AutonomyAI). The kill trigger ("fewer than 4 at 20 interviews") does not fire; the target of 15 at 50 is not met because willingness to pay is unobservable. Proxy (harness-caused failure with material cost described): 13.
- **Replay counter.** Zero stated; the kill trigger cannot be evaluated. Proxy: content cannot leave in 19 cases (3 stated), and 7 already run agents in customer-controlled sandboxes. Not used to pass the gate.
- **Design-partner counter.** Zero confirmed, one maybe; a list of 18 candidates with a public owner and unsolved pain in our scope is in `00-counters.md` §3.
- **Report-only counters.** Non-model attribution share, trace-routing share, budget-owner distribution: reported from the cases and the corpus with grades.

## 3. What this changes in the plan
1. **Phase 1 opens with live conversations, not a full discovery program.** Ten to twenty conversations aimed only at Q18–Q24 (pay, replay hosting, design partnership), drawn from the candidate list, in weeks 1–4 of Phase 1. The desk cases replace the other forty minutes of each interview.
2. **The wedge is sharper than PLAN-01 states.** Vendors gate their own harness; nobody gates the customer's harness surface (rules files, hooks, permission policy, MCP configuration, model and effort defaults). The regression gate's first target is that surface, with the vendor-shipped update as the second trigger ("did the 2.1.x release change my fleet's behaviour").
3. **Datadog is the incumbent to position against, not LangSmith.** Its Agent Console ingests Claude Code OTel, prices waste per session, flags three patterns and pushes hooks org-wide. It has no per-run reconstruction, no incident clustering, no first-divergence, no replay and no CI gate on harness changes; PLAN-07 positioning and the teardown are updated to say so.
4. **Detector backlog from the public record** (SPEC-03): hook-did-not-fire (Elastic, corpus), deny-rule bypass via subagent, instruction truncation, fabricated success, compaction-induced rework, cost under-report, side-effect-then-timeout double execution (LangGraph 9185), runaway subagent fan-out. Each has a public reproduction to anchor the Phase 1 benchmark.
5. **Standards path is confirmed by vendors already on OTel.** GitHub follows the OTel GenAI conventions explicitly; Gemini CLI, Claude Code and Codex emit `gen_ai.*`; Factory emits OTLP under its own names. The PLAN-06 sequence stands, and Factory and Salesforce are the first two vendor-track conversations about emitting the `harness.*` registry.
6. **Build-versus-buy risk is visible.** Uber, LinkedIn, NVIDIA, Datadog and Spotify build these layers in-house. PLAN-09 gains a named risk: the largest fleets are references, not customers, until the open recorder is the cheaper path.

## 4. Limits of this synthesis
Survivorship (only failures someone chose to write up), search-snippet provenance for most non-GitHub sources (a second pass re-confirmed 48 survey figures and corrected the MAST split), class labels in the corpus assigned by us, no reaction counts, Cursor and Windsurf absent from the corpus, and two cases (Goldman Sachs, Doctolib) left at grade C. Nothing here is a count of customers; it is a map of where the public record agrees with the thesis and where it is silent.
